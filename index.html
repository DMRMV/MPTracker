EU synthetic polymer microparticle restriction (European Union)

Full nameCommission Regulation (EU) 2023/2055 of 25 September 2023 amending Annex XVII to REACH (Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006) as regards synthetic polymer microparticles (new entry 78)
SectorsChemicals, Cosmetics, Health & med. devices, Environment & water
ReferenceReg. (EU) 2023/2055 — REACH Annex XVII entry 78
RequiresBans placing on the market synthetic polymer microparticles (<5 mm, insoluble, non-degradable) on their own or intentionally added at >=0.01% by weight, with staggered transition periods by product type; imposes labelling, instructions-for-use and annual ECHA emission reporting duties. Affects manufacturers and users of polymers, cosmetics, detergents, agrochemicals, medical devices and sports-surface infill.
Key datesIn force 17 Oct 2023 (loose glitter, microbeads banned). IFU/disposal info 17 Oct 2025. First ECHA reports 31 May 2026 (pellet/industrial sites), 31 May 2027 (others). IVD instructions 17 Oct 2026. Rinse-off cosmetics 17 Oct 2027; detergents, waxes, polishes, air care, fertilisers 17 Oct 2028; leave-on cosmetics, encapsulated fragrance, medical devices 17 Oct 2029; plant protection, seeds, biocides, sports infill 17 Oct 2031; lip/nail/make-up 17 Oct 2035.
DisputesNo delay to transition dates found. Amended by Reg. (EU) 2026/1168 (see EU02) — broadened medicinal-product and new PPORD derogations; narrowed solid-matrix derogation from 22 Jun 2028.
CaveatMedical devices under Reg. (EU) 2017/745 are restricted from 17 Oct 2029 unless they contain microbeads; medicinal products, IVDs (instructions only), food additives and food/feed are derogated.

The first deadline (31 May 2026, for pellet/flake/powder users in plastics manufacturing) has passed; the second is 31 May 2027 for other industrial users and suppliers.

Full nameCommission Regulation (EU) 2026/1168 of 1 June 2026 amending Annex XVII to REACH as regards synthetic polymer microparticles
SectorsChemicals, Pharma
ReferenceReg. (EU) 2026/1168
RequiresClarifies and adjusts derogations in entry 78: wider exemption for medicinal products used in clinical trials and pre-clinical testing, a new research (PPORD) exemption, and a tighter solid-matrix exemption. Affects pharma R&D, research bodies and article producers relying on the solid-matrix derogation.
Key datesAdopted 1 Jun 2026; published 2 Jun 2026; in force 22 Jun 2026. Medicinal-product (incl. clinical trials) and PPORD (<=1 t/yr) derogations apply retroactively from 17 Oct 2023. Narrowed solid-matrix derogation (end uses >=1 year) applies from 22 Jun 2028.
DisputesNew amending act (not a delay). The EUR-Lex metadata field 'first date of entry into force' shows 22 Jun 2028; secondary sources give 22 Jun 2026 as entry into force and 22 Jun 2028 as application of para 5(c).
CaveatParagraph-level application dates taken from secondary summaries consistent with the EUR-Lex record; re-read the OJ text before relying on them.

EU plastic pellet loss regulation (European Union)

Full nameRegulation (EU) 2025/2365 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 November 2025 on preventing plastic pellet losses to reduce microplastic pollution
SectorsChemicals, Environment & water
ReferenceReg. (EU) 2025/2365
RequiresOperators handling >5 t of pellets per year must keep a risk-management plan, prevent and contain spills, train staff, self-declare and (above 1,500 t/yr) obtain third-party certification; EU and non-EU carriers face packaging, labelling and stowage duties. Affects polymer producers, converters, logistics, recyclers and shipping.
Key datesAdopted 12 Nov 2025; OJ 26 Nov 2025; first obligations (prevention, clean-up, training) from 16 Dec 2025. General application 17 Dec 2027; certification: large operators (>=1,500 t/yr) by 17 Dec 2027, medium by 17 Dec 2028, small by 17 Dec 2030. Sea-transport duties from 17 Dec 2028. First Member State report 1 Jan 2030; evaluation 2033.
DisputesNone identified.
Milestones2025-12-16: Regulation in force; first obligations
2027-12-17: General application; large-site certification
2028-12-17: Medium sites; sea-vessel carrier obligations
Latest check: Obligations for EU and non-EU sea-vessel carriers apply from 17 Dec 2028, a year after general application (17 Dec 2027). The ≥5 t/yr threshold and certification for sites ≥1,500 t are confirmed.

RequiresTightens urban wastewater treatment, adds quaternary (micropollutant) treatment funded by an EPR scheme on medicinal and cosmetic product producers, and requires monitoring of microplastics in wastewater and sludge. Affects water utilities, municipalities, pharma and cosmetics producers.

Implications for labs and instrument vendors

Two measurement paradigms, and regulators are split

EU06 DWD methodCA01ISO 16094-2/-3NX11/NX12

The drinking-water rules written so far (the EU delegated decision, California's SOPs, China's draft HJ spec and T/CAEPI 109) all specify particle counts by µ-FTIR or Raman. Thermo-analytical, mass-based methods are now catching up: ISO/FDIS 16094-3 for drinking water, ASTM D8401-24, ISO/DIS 4484-4 for textiles and the TRWP technical specs. Expect labs to be asked for both: counts for compliance with existing methods, and mass for exposure, source attribution and tyre wear.

Instrument / consumable angle: py-GC-MS and TED-GC-MS have a route into formal methods via 16094-3. Position them as complementary to µ-FTIR/Raman/LDIR, not competing. Key selling points: polymer-specific mass, no lower size cut-off from optics, and polymers that IR cannot easily see (rubber/SBR, PVC in mixtures).

Drinking water: watch lists and candidate lists, not limits

No jurisdiction has a microplastics MCL. The EU has a method and a watch-list route, the US has CCL 6 (final expected ~Nov 2026) but excluded MPs from UCMR 6 for lack of a validated method, and California's Phase 2 is unconfirmed. Michigan, New Jersey, Korea and Taiwan are all moving toward monitoring. The common blocker everywhere is method validation.

Instrument / consumable angle: Demand will be for validated, high-throughput workflows: large-volume sampling (≥1,000 L), cascade filters, contamination-controlled prep, and automated spectral imaging. Interlab-study and reference-material support is a differentiator; KOLAS accreditation in Korea shows labs are already accrediting.

Wastewater and sludge: the high-solids problem

EU05 UWWTDEU13 FPRASTM D8332/D8333

The recast UWWTD brings microplastics monitoring at treatment plants and sludge, and EU fertiliser and sludge rules pull in polymer criteria. Influent, effluent and biosolids are high in solids and organics, so digestion and density separation dominate the uncertainty.
